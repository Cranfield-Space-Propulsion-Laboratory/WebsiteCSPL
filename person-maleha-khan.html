Maleha Khan

PhD Researcher

PhD Researcher · Cranfield Space Centre

Maleha Khan is a chemical propulsion PhD researcher and qualified pilot specialising in hydrogen propulsion technologies for future space exploration.

A graduate of the University of Southampton with a Master's degree in Aeronautics and Astronautics, specialising in Spacecraft Engineering, Maleha is currently undertaking a fully funded PhD within the UK's Rocketry Research, Teaching and Training (R2T2) programme.

Her research focuses on the development of cryo-compressed hydrogen storage systems for long-duration space propulsion. Her work aims to address the challenges of storing and delivering hydrogen as a rocket propellant, contributing towards reusable lunar spacecraft and sustainable exploration beyond Earth.

Alongside her academic research, Maleha has gained experience with the UK Space Agency and participated in the International Space University's Space Studies Programme in South Korea. She also served as an Acting Pilot Officer in the Royal Air Force University Air Squadron, gaining extensive leadership experience while pursuing her passion for aviation.

Maleha is an advocate for women in STEM, education and breaking traditional stereotypes. She has competed in Miss World England and uses her platform to encourage young people, particularly women, to pursue ambitious careers in science, technology and aerospace.
