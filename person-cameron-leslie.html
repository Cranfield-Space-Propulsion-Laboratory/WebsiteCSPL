Cameron Leslie

PhD Researcher

PhD Researcher & CSPL Lab Manager · Cranfield Space Centre

Research Overview

Cameron is a final-year PhD researcher at Cranfield University working on atmosphere-breathing electric propulsion (ABEP). His primary research area is active intake systems that uses cryogenics to collect and store atmospheric gases for use with ABEP, also known as Cryogenic Propellant Regeneration. His PhD, funded by ESA under the Open Space Innovation Platform, aims to demonstrate the feasibility of such systems with the first experimental prototype: PHROST, and through numerical simulations of typical mission scenarios.

Background

Cameron got his BEng in Aeronautical Engineering at the University of Glasgow (2019) before coming to Cranfield for an MSc in Astronautics and Space Engineering (2021). His thesis proposed the initial concept of Cryogenic Propellant Regneration.

Experience

Prior to starting his doctoral research, Cameron spent two years as a Research Assistant on a range of projects, including:

  • Space-to-space Imaging: Lead developer for HySim: a hyprespectral space-to-space imaging simulation software for space servicing inspection missions in collaboration with High Earth Orbit Robotics and Astroscale.
  • Atmosphere-Breathing Electric Propulsion: Feasibilty studies on Cryogenic Propellant Regeneration funded by the UK Space Agency.
  • Orbital Robotics: Lead mechanical design and control system engineer for MARIO: Multi Armed Robotic for In-orbit Operations with ASTRALab during ESA's ISAAC project.

Research Interests

  • Atmosphere-breathing electric propulsion
  • Very low Earth orbit (VLEO) missions
  • Electric propulsion
  • Orbital Robotics
